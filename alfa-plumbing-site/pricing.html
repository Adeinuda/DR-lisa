How a price gets set

Six things that move a Baytown quote.

Knowing which of these applies to you is most of the reason to call before you buy a part online.

  1. Fuel and type. Electric tank, gas tank, electric point-of-use or gas tankless — the equipment and venting differ by hundreds of dollars, not tens.
  2. Age of the existing unit. Under ten years old usually means repair. Ten to fifteen means the repair money is better spent on a replacement that is quoted while we are there.
  3. Access. A heater in a garage corner is one job; one in an attic, a closet with no pan, or a crawlspace is longer work and more hands.
  4. Code items found on the way. Expansion tanks, T&P piping to code, seismic straps, pans, drain lines, fresh air intake. Each is cheap alone and they are quoted as options, not surprises.
  5. Pipe and material on a repipe. Copper, PEX and CPVC differ in cost and in how much wall gets opened; patching is a separate line item by choice, not necessity.
  6. Depth of the line for underground work. A sewer lateral under a driveway and one under turf are different excavations. Camera first — then the right number.

Money questions

The two we get asked first.

The rest are on the FAQ page.

Do you charge for estimates?
No. Walk-through estimates for repipes, remodels, sewer repairs and water heater replacements are free — you only pay for work you approve.
Can I use a toilet that won't flush or won't stop running?
A running toilet is usually a flapper that needs replacing and is safe to use while you wait. A toilet that will not drain is a different problem: if multiple drains in the house are slow or gurgling, stop running water and call, because it points to the main line.
