What the company was for

Servando Perez started the company in 2003 in Baytown with a simple complaint about the trade: plumbers who quoted a new water heater because they had not looked at the $20 part that had failed. The business was built to be the opposite of that — a licensed shop that explains the diagnosis first, prices the work before it starts, and guarantees the labour it does.

Twenty-plus years later the pattern is the same. Service calls, replacements, repipes, sewer linings, septic permits, remodel rough-ins and after-hours emergencies, mostly for people in Baytown, Deer Park, La Porte, Pasadena and the ship-channel neighbourhoods who found us through a neighbour.

What “licensed and insured” means here

Alfa is a Texas Master Plumber operation, licensed and insured, family-owned. That matters in three specific places: work that needs a county permit (septic replacement, sewer, gas conversions) gets the permit pulled and the as-built filed; insurance-backed crews are in your house; and the 100% satisfaction guarantee comes from the licensed company that did the work, not a subcontractor.

One number, one team

(713) 992-9257 is the shop line and info@alfaplumbingservices.com is the inbox. There is no dispatch service in the middle, which is also why the same person who quoted your job can tell you what happened to it.

The guarantee, in plain words

Alfa offers a 100% satisfaction guarantee with a money-back clause on the work it performs. In practice it means: if the part we replaced is not the reason it is still doing the thing, you do not pay for the wrong guess. New-customer terms currently include 10% off your first visit over $300 on a first visit over $300.

Who we work for

  • Homeowners — repair, replacement and maintenance on occupied houses.
  • Buyers, sellers and agents — inspections and repairs between contract and closing, on the clock the transaction sets.
  • Property managers — tenant calls, after-hours dispatch and documented scopes.
  • Restoration companies — water-damage scoping, photographs and pricing for the claim.
  • Builders and remodelers — rough-in and finish plumbing with inspections coordinated.
  • Commercial accounts — light commercial and small business, 24-hour dispatch.

Why the estimate comes before the price

Anything that opens a wall, replaces a tank or runs new pipe gets looked at first, because that is the only way the number on the invoice matches the number on the estimate. That is also why the walk-through estimate is free on that work, and why the small stuff — drips, flappers, hoses, jams — gets booked without a sales conversation at all.

How a job runs

Five steps, every time.

Same sequence for a $60 flapper and a $12,000 repipe. It is the reason the estimate holds.

  1. Tell us the symptom. Phone, text or the request form. Photographs and a short description cut the diagnosis in half, so send them.
  2. Diagnosis and a price. We test the cheap causes first and show you what we found. New work gets a walk-through estimate, free.
  3. Approval before tools come out. Nothing gets opened, cut or replaced that you have not agreed to and priced.
  4. The fix, and what else we saw. Work done while you are still in the house, plus the honest note about the thing that is not broken yet.
  5. Guaranteed, and written down. 100% workmanship guarantee, warranty paperwork left with you, and a record of the parts and settings we used.

Principles

Four rules that make the phone call shorter.

They also make some jobs smaller than the competition would like. We prefer it that way.

Explain before you sell.

If a $20 part fixes it, that is the repair. The company publishes DIY guides that save customers money on service calls — that is the whole culture of the shop.

Location before demolition.

Camera, pressure test or acoustic trace first. Walls and yards get opened once, at the right place.

One price, agreed in advance.

Free walk-through estimates on repipes, remodels, sewer repair and water heater replacements, and the invoice matches them.

Leave it cleaner than we found it.

Drop cloths, boot covers, haul-away of the old unit and the packaging, and a look at the ceiling below the work before we leave.
